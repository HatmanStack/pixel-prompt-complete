## Why this exists

Pixel Prompt displays a public gallery of images generated by its users on free
and signed-out tiers, and a public feed of the prompts that produced them.
Generations on a paid plan are private and appear in neither. Either way we
store user-generated content, which makes us a host: that brings an obligation
to act on copyright complaints and, if we do it properly, the safe-harbour
protection of 17 U.S.C. § 512.

Notices are actionable against private content too. Privacy limits who can see
material; it does not make hosting an infringing copy lawful.

**Safe harbour is not automatic.** It requires a designated agent registered
with the US Copyright Office, a published process, and a repeat-infringer
policy that we actually apply. See the checklist at the end.

## Filing a takedown notice

Send a written notice to the agent above including all six elements required by
17 U.S.C. § 512(c)(3):

1. Your physical or electronic signature, as the copyright owner or someone
   authorised to act for them.
2. Identification of the copyrighted work you claim is infringed.
3. Identification of the material you claim is infringing, **with enough detail
   for us to find it** — the gallery URL or image URL, not just a description.
4. Your contact details: address, telephone number, and email.
5. A statement that you have a good-faith belief the use is not authorised by
   the copyright owner, its agent, or the law.
6. A statement, **under penalty of perjury**, that the information in the
   notice is accurate and that you are authorised to act for the owner.

An incomplete notice may not be actionable. We may ask you for the missing
parts.

**Misrepresentation carries liability.** Under § 512(f), knowingly
misrepresenting that material is infringing can make you liable for damages,
including costs and attorneys' fees. Consider whether the use is fair use
before filing.

## Counter-notice

If your content was removed and you believe that was a mistake or a
misidentification, send a counter-notice to the agent above containing:

1. Your physical or electronic signature.
2. Identification of the removed material and where it appeared.
3. A statement **under penalty of perjury** that you have a good-faith belief
   it was removed as a result of mistake or misidentification.
4. Your name, address, and telephone number, and a statement consenting to the
   jurisdiction of the Federal District Court for your district, or for
   {{JURISDICTION}} if you are outside the United States, and that you will
   accept service of process from the person who filed the notice.

If we receive a valid counter-notice we forward it to the complainant. Unless
they notify us within **10 business days** that they have filed a court action
seeking to restrain you, **we will restore the material between 10 and 14
business days** after receiving your counter-notice. This is what
17 U.S.C. § 512(g) requires of us, not something we do at our discretion.

**Your contact details are passed to the complainant.** That is what the
statute requires. Consider it before filing.

## A note specific to AI-generated images

Images here are produced by AI models in response to user prompts. That does not
put them outside copyright law.

- A generated image may substantially resemble a protected work, particularly
  where a prompt names a character, brand, or specific work.
- Some of our providers state that their models are trained on licensed or
  public-domain data and offer indemnification. **That is a commitment between
  those providers and us, not a defence available to you, and it does not cover
  images from the other models.**
- We do not adjudicate whether a generated image is infringing or fair use. We
  process notices as the statute requires.

Prompting deliberately to reproduce a protected work also violates our
[Acceptable Use Policy](acceptable-use-policy.md).
